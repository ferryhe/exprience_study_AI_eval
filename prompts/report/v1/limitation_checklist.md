# Required limitation and governance coverage

- Aggregated public data cannot support policy-level causal conclusions.
- Company identities, underwriting practices, product mixes, and contributions are unavailable or anonymized.
- Total, Core, and Modern results depend on frozen eligibility definitions and null handling.
- Published expected-death fields are authoritative; XML rates are lower-precision independent checks.
- Count and amount A/E use different exposure and expected bases and are not interchangeable.
- Mortality-improvement and non-improvement results must be labeled distinctly.
- Sparse or low-credibility cells must not be ranked or generalized without qualification.
- Multiple comparisons, model selection, and exploratory segmentation can create false discoveries.
- Data-collection methodology changed in 2018-2019 and affects time-trend interpretation.
- Explanatory models do not replace reconciled deterministic totals and do not establish causation.
- Results require actuarial judgment and are not pricing, reserving, underwriting, or regulatory advice.
- Numerical reconciliation, reproducibility checks, and independent actuarial review are release gates.
- AI-generated narrative may contain errors; only validated evidence artifacts are authoritative.
