# Report style

- Write formal, concise US English for actuarial practitioners and technically informed stakeholders.
- Lead sections with the supported finding, then explain evidence, interpretation, and limitations.
- Use `A/E` only after defining actual-to-expected. Express ratios consistently and preserve evidence rounding.
- Distinguish count basis from amount basis and with-MI from without-MI in every affected claim.
- Avoid promotional language, provider comparisons inside the research narrative, false precision, causal wording, and unsupported recommendations.
- Do not imitate SOA report wording. Functional and analytical comparability is the target, not textual copying.
- Keep tables and charts in exhibits; narrative should explain material patterns rather than enumerate every value.
- Use neutral references such as “the study,” “the validated evidence,” and registered population names.
